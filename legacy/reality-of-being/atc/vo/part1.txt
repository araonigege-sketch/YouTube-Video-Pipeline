So you think you could be an air traffic controller.

It’s an appealing thought. You sit in a dim room in front of a glowing scope. You speak in that calm, clipped, unbothered voice. You hold hundreds of lives in your hands and your pulse never moves. And they pay you for it. Really pay you. A typical air traffic controller in America earns just over $144,000 a year. The ones at the busy centers clear $185,000, and with enough overtime, past $250,000. You don’t even strictly need a college degree. Just you, a headset, and nerves of steel.

Small problem. You wouldn’t last a week.

Not because you’re weak. Because almost nobody can. The job you’re picturing, the calm voice and the six-figure federal paycheck, is real. What you’re not picturing is the thing underneath it. By the end of this video, you’ll understand why that enormous salary isn’t a reward. It’s a warning label. And you’ll understand what this job quietly does to the people who do survive it, the thing that follows them out of the tower, into their cars, into their marriages, and never quite lets go.

Okay. Let’s start with whether they’d even let you in.

You can’t even begin this career after you turn 31. That’s a hard federal cutoff, and it exists for a brutal little reason we’ll come back to. Say you make it under the wire. You get shipped to the FAA Academy in Oklahoma City. And here’s the first number that should scare you. In 2024, more than 30% of trainees washed out. When the Department of Transportation’s inspector general dug into that failure rate, the FAA didn’t even argue. The agency admitted, flat out, that its own washout rate was unacceptable.
