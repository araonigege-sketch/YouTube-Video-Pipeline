# What Really Destroyed the Library of Alexandria

## The Greatest Library on Earth
[stock: ancient stone columns ruins]
[stock: old scrolls manuscripts]
Two thousand years ago, on the edge of the Mediterranean, a city tried to collect every book in the world. Ships arriving in Alexandria were searched, and any scrolls on board were copied for the Library.

[stock: candle light old books]
At its height, it may have held hundreds of thousands of scrolls. Mathematics, medicine, poetry, astronomy. The memory of the ancient world, in one place.

## The Fire That Wasn't
[card: 48 BC — Julius Caesar besieges Alexandria]
Most people think the Library burned in a single, dramatic night. In 48 BC, Julius Caesar set fire to ships in the harbor, and the flames spread into the city.

[stock: fire flames dark slow motion]
But the Library didn't vanish then. Scholars were still working there decades later.

## A Slow Death
[stock: dust light rays abandoned room]
The truth is quieter, and more unsettling. Funding dried up. Scholars were expelled by rulers who saw them as a threat. Scrolls rotted, and nobody paid to copy them.

[stock: desert sand blowing]
The greatest library in history wasn't destroyed by one fire. It was abandoned, one forgotten scroll at a time.
